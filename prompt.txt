## Mandatory evidence classifications

Use the following classifications consistently.

### Verified product evidence

Information directly supported by:

- locally preserved listing text;
- a listing screenshot;
- an original product photograph;
- a readable PCB, component, terminal, or connector marking;
- a seller statement preserved with its source location.

### External technical evidence

Information supported by an official manual, standard, academic publication, textbook, or appropriate technical source.

External technical evidence must not be presented as proof of the commercial card’s exact implementation.

### Engineering inference

A technically reasonable interpretation derived from verified evidence and accepted engineering practice.

Every inference must be explicitly labeled as an inference.

### Proposed reference design

A future design choice for the project’s conceptual architecture or software simulator.

Do not present proposed-design decisions as facts about the commercial product.

### Unknown or unresolved

Information that cannot currently be established from the available evidence.

Unknowns must be recorded explicitly rather than replaced with assumptions.

## Scientific and wording restrictions

Do not claim that the commercial card contains any of the following unless a clear marking or authoritative product document proves it:

- an ARM processor;
- an STM32 processor;
- an STM32F103;
- any specific processor family;
- a specific relay or output-driver model;
- a specific memory capacity;
- a specific RFID protocol;
- a specific Wiegand frame format;
- a specific voltage, current, or contact rating;
- a specific communication interface;
- a specific firmware architecture.

Do not infer exact PCB traces, hidden layers, component values, or internal circuitry from low-resolution images.

Do not describe the project as exact reverse engineering of the commercial product.

Do not state that the visible output channels directly control elevator motors, brakes, doors, or passenger-safety functions.

Treat the studied card only as an access-authorization layer.

Use qualified wording such as:

- “The listing advertises...”
- “The marking visible in image IMG-001 reads...”
- “This may indicate..., but the function is not verified.”
- “The exact implementation is unknown.”
- “The later reference design may model this as..., but this is not a verified property of the commercial card.”
